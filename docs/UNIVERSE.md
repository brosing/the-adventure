# Text-Based Game Universe Documentation
> Universe Design Guide v1.0

---

# Manifesto

People today don't hate reading.

They simply forgot how to enjoy it.

This project exists to help them rediscover that joy.

Not through textbooks.

Not through educational games.

But through beautiful stories.

Every game should leave the player slightly more patient.

Slightly more attentive.

Slightly more human.

---

# Vision

Create a series of philosophical text-based games that gradually transform non-readers into passionate readers.

The games should never feel like educational software.

Instead, they should feel like timeless fairy tales where every new game quietly teaches a more advanced way of experiencing stories.

---

# Product Mission

> Help people fall in love with reading again through text-based games.

The progression is **not about becoming stronger.**

It is about becoming a better reader.

Every new title increases the player's reading capability without them realizing it.

---

# Narrative Mission

> Tell timeless philosophical fairy tales that help people reclaim what modern life quietly steals away.

The four games form a single quiet argument.

The world is louder than it has ever been.

Not because people became cruel.

Because attention became cheap.

Doomscrolling is the symptom. The series is the long, slow antidote.

Each story treats one layer of the wound:

- **Game I** — the noise outside. Reclaim your solitude.
- **Game II** — the noise inside. Reclaim your focus.
- **Game III** — the noise of time. Reclaim your presence.
- **Game IV** — the noise of the crowd. Reclaim your wisdom.

None of this is ever said aloud in the games.

It is only felt.

The games should **ask questions**, not provide answers.

Like:

- The Little Prince
- Momo

The player should leave with reflections instead of lessons.

---

# Universe Identity

## Genre

Modern Philosophical Fairy Tale Universe

A shared universe where each story explores one forgotten human virtue.

Stories are standalone.

Characters occasionally cross paths.

Locations return.

Legends evolve.

The world remembers.

---

# Core Design Principles

## Story First

Gameplay always exists to deepen the story.

Never the opposite.

---

## Philosophy Before Lore

Lore should support philosophy.

Never replace it.

Good:

> The Quiet Whale symbolizes forgotten solitude.

Bad:

> Seven kingdoms fought for magical crystals.

The universe should never become fantasy for fantasy's sake.

---

## Quiet Worldbuilding

Avoid large exposition dumps.

Players should slowly discover the world through:

- conversations
- letters
- journals
- objects
- myths
- recurring places

---

## Magical Realism

Magic exists naturally.

Nobody explains it scientifically.

Examples:

- Silence can be collected.
- Memories become birds.
- Time becomes heavier.
- Lamps remember their owners.
- Roads disappear.
- Trees whisper.

Characters simply accept these things.

---

# Product Progression

Each game teaches a higher level of reading.

The player never realizes they are being taught.

```
Game I   →  Read + Attentive
              Stay with the story. Notice what is quiet.

Game II  →  Connective
              Hold two things at once. Notice they touch.

Game III →  Explorative
              Navigate a world. Choose what to attend to.

Game IV  →  Systemic
              Live inside the story. Tend it like a world.
```

---

# Narrative Progression

Each story reclaims something modern life takes.

```
Game I   →  Solitude
              The noise is outside. Learn to be alone without loneliness.

Game II  →  Focus
              The noise is inside. Learn to give one thing your whole attention.

Game III →  Presence
              The noise is time itself. Learn to be fully here.

Game IV  →  Phronesis
              Practical wisdom. Learn what tending a world actually requires.
```

Gameplay teaches reading.

Story teaches living.

Neither announces itself.

# Character Progression

Never use combat stats.

Use human qualities.

The qualities a player might grow:

- Attention
- Curiosity
- Empathy
- Patience
- Memory
- Resolve

Stats grow through behavior, never announced.

Read something carefully → Attention deepens.

Help a stranger without reward → Empathy grows.

Rush a conversation → nothing changes.

This system belongs fully to Game IV.

Earlier games carry only traces of it.

# World Design

The universe should feel small but deep.

Recurring locations:

Already canon, from Game I:

- Bellwood
- The willow at the edge of town
- The Keeper's cottage beyond the map
- The Market of Loud Things
- The City That Forgot How to Listen, and its dry fountain
- The Listening Forest
- The Room of Unspoken Words
- The Sea of Still Waters
- The Mountain of Forgotten Dreams
- The Silence Wells
- The First Quiet
- The listening houses

Introduced in Game III (Varemoor), carried into Game IV:

- Lighthouse
- Bakery
- Library
- Ferry Station
- Tea House (with a listening house above it)

Players gradually become familiar with them across games.

A place returns changed by time, or seen from another person's angle.

Never as a souvenir.

---

# Writing Style

Inspired by:

- The Little Prince
- Momo

Writing should be:

- simple
- poetic
- visual
- timeless
- emotional
- reflective

Avoid:

- lengthy exposition
- complicated fantasy terminology
- hard magic systems
- modern slang
- preaching

Always show.

Rarely explain.

---

# Dialogue Style

Dialogue should feel quiet.

Characters rarely speak long paragraphs.

Small sentences often carry larger meanings.

Example

Bad

> You should spend less time using your Chatterbox because solitude is important.

Good

> "Do you hear that?"

> "Hear what?"

> "Exactly."

---

# Golden Rule

Every mechanic must answer one question:

> Does this help the player engage more deeply with the story?

If the answer is no,

remove it.
