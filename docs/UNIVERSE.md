# Text-Based Game Universe Documentation
> Universe Design Guide v1.0

---

# Manifesto

People today don't hate reading.

They simply forgot how to enjoy it.

This project exists to help them rediscover that joy.

Not through textbooks.

Not through educational games.

But through beautiful stories.

Every game should leave the player slightly more patient.

Slightly more attentive.

Slightly more human.

---

# Vision

Create a series of philosophical text-based games that gradually transform non-readers into passionate readers.

The games should never feel like educational software.

Instead, they should feel like timeless fairy tales where every new game quietly teaches a more advanced way of experiencing stories.

---

# Product Mission

> Help people fall in love with reading again through text-based games.

The progression is **not about becoming stronger.**

It is about becoming a better reader.

Every new title increases the player's reading capability without them realizing it.

---

# Narrative Mission

> Tell timeless philosophical fairy tales that help people rediscover what modern life quietly steals away.

Every story should explore one core human value.

Examples:

- Solitude
- Focus
- Presence
- Belonging

The games should **ask questions**, not provide answers.

Like:

- The Little Prince
- Momo

The player should leave with reflections instead of lessons.

---

# Universe Identity

## Genre

Modern Philosophical Fairy Tale Universe

A shared universe where each story explores one forgotten human virtue.

Stories are standalone.

Characters occasionally cross paths.

Locations return.

Legends evolve.

The world remembers.

---

# Core Design Principles

## Story First

Gameplay always exists to deepen the story.

Never the opposite.

---

## Philosophy Before Lore

Lore should support philosophy.

Never replace it.

Good:

> The Quiet Whale symbolizes forgotten solitude.

Bad:

> Seven kingdoms fought for magical crystals.

The universe should never become fantasy for fantasy's sake.

---

## Quiet Worldbuilding

Avoid large exposition dumps.

Players should slowly discover the world through:

- conversations
- letters
- journals
- objects
- myths
- recurring places

---

## Magical Realism

Magic exists naturally.

Nobody explains it scientifically.

Examples:

- Silence can be collected.
- Memories become birds.
- Time becomes heavier.
- Lamps remember their owners.
- Roads disappear.
- Trees whisper.

Characters simply accept these things.

---

# Character Progression

Never use combat stats.

Use human qualities.

Examples:

- Attention
- Curiosity
- Empathy
- Patience
- Memory
- Resolve

Stats grow through behavior.

Example

Read books

↓

Attention +

Help strangers

↓

Empathy +

Rush conversations

↓

No growth

# World Design

The universe should feel small but deep.

Recurring locations:

Already canon, from Game I:

- Bellwood
- The willow at the edge of town
- The Keeper's cottage beyond the map
- The Market of Loud Things
- The City That Forgot How to Listen, and its dry fountain
- The Listening Forest
- The Room of Unspoken Words
- The Sea of Still Waters
- The Mountain of Forgotten Dreams
- The Silence Wells
- The First Quiet
- The listening houses

Planned, not yet written:

- Lighthouse
- Bakery
- Library
- Station
- Tea House

Players gradually become familiar with them across games.

A place returns changed by time, or seen from another person's angle.

Never as a souvenir.

---

# Writing Style

Inspired by:

- The Little Prince
- Momo

Writing should be:

- simple
- poetic
- visual
- timeless
- emotional
- reflective

Avoid:

- lengthy exposition
- complicated fantasy terminology
- hard magic systems
- modern slang
- preaching

Always show.

Rarely explain.

---

# Dialogue Style

Dialogue should feel quiet.

Characters rarely speak long paragraphs.

Small sentences often carry larger meanings.

Example

Bad

> You should spend less time using your Chatterbox because solitude is important.

Good

> "Do you hear that?"

> "Hear what?"

> "Exactly."

---

# Golden Rule

Every mechanic must answer one question:

> Does this help the player engage more deeply with the story?

If the answer is no,

remove it.
