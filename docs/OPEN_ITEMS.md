### Game IV — Open-world or open-order?

**The gap:** The current mechanics list has seven systems: open world, quest system, reputation, character progression, multiple endings, hidden quests, lore collection. That's a very large scope for a story-first game.

**The alternative:** *Open-order* — a small set of deeply written, self-contained stories (the letter, the garden, the shop, the well) that the player can approach in any sequence. Each is its own chapter. The scope stays intimate. The prose quality stays Game I-level.

**The question:** Full open-world RPG, or intimate open-order narrative?

Each system in the current list should answer the Golden Rule: *Does this help the player engage more deeply with the story?* The ones most at risk are: reputation, character progression, lore collection.

**Where to write the answer:** Update `### New Mechanics` in `SERIES_OVERVIEW/GAME_4.md`.

### Game IV — How do the three endings interact with the tending-quests?

**The gap:** The three ending answers are described (tell them the way / tell them it doesn't exist / tell them how to make one where they stand). But it's not clear how the player arrives at them.

**Option A — Always available:** All three answers are always presented, regardless of what the player did. Consistent with the Choice Philosophy (choices change meaning, not plot).

**Option B — Earned through tending:** The player can only speak certain answers if they have lived a certain way. The third answer (the hardest) requires having actually built something — reopened a listening house, finished someone's story, tended a well.

**Where to write the answer:** Add a `### How the Ending Works` section to `SERIES_OVERVIEW/GAME_4.md`.
