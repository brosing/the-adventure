# Fiction Chapter Review

A structured workflow for reviewing fiction the way a developmental editor would: chapter by chapter first, then across the whole manuscript. The goal is a *semantic* review — one that evaluates what the prose is doing (imagery, logic, characterization, tension, genre promise) rather than just surface grammar.

## Before you start

1. Read `references/review-rubric.md` in full. It contains the five review lenses and the specific failure patterns to look for. Do not review from memory alone.
2. Establish context with the user if not already clear:
   - What genre is this? (If cozy fantasy, apply the genre lens strictly; otherwise apply it only where the user asks for genre feedback and adapt it to their genre's promises.)
   - Which chapters are being reviewed, and are they sequential? A mid-book chapter can't be judged for "introducing" characters the reader already knows.
   - What kind of feedback do they want: big-picture developmental notes, line-level notes, or both? Default to developmental with a few illustrative line examples.
3. Read the manuscript **twice**. First pass: read as a reader, noting where you were confused, bored, delighted, or thrown out of the story. Second pass: read as an editor, applying the rubric lens by lens.

## The review process

### Pass 1 — Per-chapter review

For each chapter, evaluate against all five lenses from the rubric:

1. **Figurative language & personification** — Is imagery active or inert? Does personified nature/setting mirror character emotion, or is it decorative? Is it overdone into melodrama?
2. **Plot integrity** — Track rules, facts, and timeline as you read. Log every established rule (magic systems, world rules, physical facts like eye color or who owns what) in a running "story bible" as you go, and check later chapters against it. Classify problems as: logical inconsistency (breaks the plot), continuity error (contradicts a detail), or out-of-character moment (action unjustified by established traits).
3. **Character introduction & consistency** — For each character's *first* appearance, check it against the introduction techniques. For returning characters, check consistency with their established voice, quirks, and motivations.
4. **Tension & structural placement** — What question does this chapter raise or answer? Does tension escalate, plateau, or release — and is that appropriate for where the chapter sits in the arc?
5. **Genre promise** — Does the chapter keep the promises of its genre? For cozy fantasy: stakes stay personal, tone stays warm, community is present, setbacks don't turn grim.

### Pass 2 — Whole-manuscript synthesis

After all chapters are reviewed individually:

- **Cross-chapter continuity check.** Compare your story bible entries across chapters. Most plot holes only become visible here.
- **Arc check.** Map the inciting incident, rising action, and climax. Is the climax recognizable, placed roughly 75–95% through, emotionally satisfying, and *earned* (seeded by earlier clues rather than arriving from nowhere)? Does it answer the story's big question?
- **Character arc check.** Does the protagonist's growth accumulate across chapters, or reset?
- **Genre arc check** (cozy fantasy): does the story end warmer than it began, with struggles resolved and community strengthened?

## Report structure

ALWAYS use this template for the final review:

```
# Review: [Title]

## Overall impression
(2-4 sentences: what the story is doing well and the single biggest opportunity.)

## Chapter-by-chapter notes
### Chapter N — [chapter title or one-line summary]
**Strengths:** ...
**Figurative language:** ...
**Plot integrity:** ... (cite chapter + quote/paraphrase the contradicting passages for any hole found)
**Characters:** ...
**Tension:** ...
**Genre fit:** ... (omit this line if not reviewing against a genre)

## Whole-story findings
### Plot holes & continuity
(Numbered list. For each: type [logical inconsistency / continuity error / out-of-character], severity [breaks the story / noticeable / minor], the two conflicting passages, and a suggested fix.)
### Structure & climax
### Character arcs
### Genre promise scorecard  (if applicable — see rubric for the 5-element cozy fantasy checklist)

## Top 3 revision priorities
(Ranked, each with a concrete, actionable suggestion.)
```

## Review voice

- Be honest but warm — the tone of a trusted critique partner, not a takedown. Lead every chapter's notes with a genuine strength before problems.
- Ground every criticism in evidence: quote or closely paraphrase the passage, name the principle it violates, and suggest at least one concrete fix. Never say "the pacing is off" without saying where and why.
- Severity matters. A story-breaking logical inconsistency deserves more space than a wardrobe-level continuity slip. Say explicitly which problems are forgivable.
- If a chapter is genuinely working, say so briefly and move on. Padding a review with invented problems is worse than a short review.
- If the manuscript is long, offer to deliver the review as a document file rather than in chat.
